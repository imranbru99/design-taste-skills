# Before / after

Three comparisons for the same fictional studio, [Atelier Maren](gallery.html). Maren fires tile for architects in Portland. The "before" is the habitual AI landing page and dashboard. The "after" is the same information designed around the job.

Open [gallery.html](gallery.html) in a browser to see the visual version. The decisions are recorded here so they remain useful as text.

## 1. First screen

**Sentence.** An architect specifying a lobby uses this to judge whether Maren's tile belongs in the room, and should notice the glaze and the lead time first.

**Before.** Centered column on an indigo-to-fuchsia gradient. Eyebrow "AI-powered studio". Headline "Elevate your interiors". Subhead "Seamless. Beautiful. Timeless." Two pills: "Get Started" and "Learn More". The swap test fails open: any studio, any SaaS, any theme could wear it.

**After.** Split structure. Eyebrow names the place. The headline is a claim the studio can keep: tile fired in small batches for rooms that should still look right in twenty years. One action, "Request a firing", plus a text link, "See current work". The right side is the four glazes, labeled, not an illustration of a customer. Meta line: "18-week lead time · 4 standard glazes · Portland kiln".

**What changed.** Structure, specificity, and accent budget. The gradient, the second pill, and the adjectives left. Nothing was added to replace them except the specimen.

## 2. Capabilities

**Before.** Three equal cards titled Fast, Sustainable, and Premium. Each has a colored icon circle and two lines of gray text. The titles are adjectives. The cards are identical, so none of them can be the point.

**After.** A short list of constraints, which is what an architect actually specifies against:

- 18-week lead time, written down before you specify
- 4 glazes, mixed in the Portland kiln
- Minimum run of 40 modules
- No paint-chip matching

**What changed.** Grouping by a list and a heading instead of by boxes. The content became something only this studio would say.

## 3. Order queue

**Before.** "Welcome back, Alex" over four gradient stat cards: Orders, Revenue, Growth, Satisfaction. Green arrows. No due dates. Nothing tells Alex which order is late.

**After.** Screen type: queue. Title "Orders", action "New order". One summary line: "14 open · 3 past due · due this week". A table sorted with the late work first. Status is the word "Past due" plus a dot, in danger ink, not a colored card. Columns align. Numbers use tabular figures.

**What changed.** Density and hierarchy. The vanity metrics left. The exception (late orders) became the loudest fact.

## What we refused to invent

The gallery does not include fake customer portraits, a logo cloud, star ratings, or a testimonial from "Sarah K." Proof that is not real is not a visual improvement. The lead time and the glaze count are part of the specimen's fiction and are labeled as such in the gallery introduction.
